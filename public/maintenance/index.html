A maintenance visit is not a promise to repair everything

ENERGY STAR recommends professional preseason maintenance and provides a checklist covering controls, electrical connections, condensate drainage and system-specific heating and cooling components. Use that list to ask what a contractor’s proposed visit includes; do not assume every task is covered by every agreement. Source: ENERGY STAR Maintenance Checklist.

Ask for the applicable checklist for your exact equipment. A furnace, ducted heat pump and ductless system need not have identical tasks. Technical inspection, refrigerant work, combustion work and electrical work belong with qualified professionals, not a homeowner following a generic web checklist.

Compare agreements line by line

Questions to ask each maintenance-plan provider
TopicQuestion to put in writing
EquipmentWhich indoor and outdoor units and systems are included?
VisitsWhat visits are included, who schedules them, and what happens if one is missed?
TasksIs there an equipment-specific checklist and a record of findings after each visit?
ExclusionsAre parts, repairs, consumables or additional diagnostic work separate? How is approval obtained?
AvailabilityDoes the contract make an actual response commitment? What limitations apply?
Renewal and cancellationDoes it renew automatically? How do cancellation, transfer and unused visits work?
Records and warrantiesWhat documentation is provided, and which manufacturer or labor-warranty requirements should be checked?

This is an original comparison worksheet, not a claim about any provider’s terms. A plan does not give this guide the ability to book service or guarantee attendance.

Agree on safe owner tasks for your exact model

ENERGY STAR discusses filter care as an owner task and suggests having the contractor demonstrate it. Before doing any task, use the exact equipment manual and ask what is intended for an owner, which filter or consumable is specified and when to stop and call for help. This expansion gives no removal, cleaning, shutdown or reset procedure and does not prescribe a universal filter-change interval. Source: ENERGY STAR filter-care guidance.

For fuel-burning heating, CPSC recommends professional inspection and servicing, including relevant chimneys and flues. A maintenance contract’s name alone does not establish that those items are included. Ask the provider to identify its inspection scope. Source: CPSC CO prevention guidance.

Keep visit records, findings, approved work and warranty documents together. If a visit leads to a repair recommendation, use the AC estimate questions or heat-pump decision guide to ask what evidence supports the next step.
